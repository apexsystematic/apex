How the numbers work

The maths, in full

Conservative inputs, sourced rates, no vendor projections. Here's exactly what the calculator does with the numbers you set.

Step 1

Hours × team × 52

Your weekly hours per person across the four categories, multiplied by team size, multiplied by 52 weeks. This is the baseline annual hours your firm spends on manual work that automation can address.

Step 2

Hours × billable rate

Annual hours converted at your billable rate. Default rates are sector blended averages — Legal £180, Accounting £120, Advisory £150, Consulting £140 — adjustable via slider.

Step 3

Recoverable: 60–80%

Industry-observed range for the share of manual work that's automation-addressable. Calculator uses the 70% midpoint. Field studies show solo practitioners and small firms tend to land at the upper end.

Step 4

Stalling: linear projection

The 6/12/24 month numbers are linear projections of the annual cost. No compounding, no escalation. Real-world losses tend to be larger as workload grows — this is the floor, not the ceiling.

What's deliberately excluded: Vendor productivity claims. "Up to" figures from marketing copy. Self-reported time savings without controlled methodology. Compounding effects. Anything that couldn't be reproduced or independently verified.

A note on accuracy

This is the broad picture — not the actual number.

A calculator with seven inputs gives you a defensible range. It can't tell you which workflow in your firm leaks the most hours, which connection between which tools is worth building first, or what passes regulatory tests in your specific context. That's what the audit does.

What this calculator does well

Gives you a sourced, conservative baseline for the cost of inaction. Useful for budgeting, for partner conversations, and for deciding whether automation is worth investigating further. The number is defensible — every input is anchored to your billing reality.

What it can't tell you

Which of your specific workflows is leaking the most hours. Which connection between which tools is worth building first. Whether the highest-ROI automation in your firm passes the regulatory tests in your sector. The number above is real — but it's an average across firms like yours, not a map of yours specifically.
